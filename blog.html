Practical guides.

General starting points. Follow your component datasheets and the requirements of your own design.

A new board will not power up

A starting checklist for PCB power troubleshooting.

Inspect polarity, solder bridges, and component orientation before applying power. With the board unpowered, check for an unexpected short between supply and ground.

Use a current-limited supply at the specified input voltage. Stop if current or temperature is abnormal. Check each rail against the design before attaching other equipment.

A serial connection shows no data

Check port settings, wiring, and signal levels.

Confirm the correct port, baud rate, and framing settings. Check TX-to-RX wiring and a common reference ground where required.

Verify voltage-level compatibility before connecting devices. TTL UART and RS-232 are not interchangeable. Test one link at a time with a known-good message.

A sensor is not responding

First checks for I²C and SPI devices.

Confirm supply and logic voltage, interface mode, and pin assignment against the datasheet.

For I²C, check the address and pull-ups. For SPI, check chip select and clock mode. Start at a supported low bus speed and inspect the bus if the device remains silent.

How to report a reproducible problem

Provide the information someone else needs to reproduce your issue.

Include the objective, hardware revision, firmware version, wiring, exact symptoms, expected behavior, and steps to reproduce.

Share relevant logs and what you have already tried. Remove secrets, personal information, and proprietary files before posting publicly.
